I will detour for **ramen**, **pho**, or **bun bo hue** when someone else is simmering for hours. Same when I am out at a shop that does it right. Glad those bowls exist. I still hate the price. A shop bowl of beef noodle soup costs the same as a **kilo of stew beef cubes**. That is the part that gets me.

At home I want something close enough. Rich enough to sip, not a packet. No charred bones. No overnight stock. No me standing over a pot for three hours.

**Minimum effort.** One kilo in. **6-8 bowls** out, plus broth for later. The **Instant Pot** carries the simmer while I am somewhere else.

This is the version I actually make - one **8-quart** run. Not a copy of any of the above. No spice paste from scratch either.

I joined my wife at the salon once. A Chinese restaurant next door had beef noodle soup. I ordered a bowl while I waited. Their broth disappointed me. Mine at home tastes better.

**Rough-chop** yellow onion, tomatoes, and ginger. **1 kg cubed beef** from the package.

**2.4 L** water, **3 beef bouillon cubes**, **light soy**, **Worcestershire** at the start. No wine. Blanch and brown when I have **10-15 minutes** extra.

Dump, stir once, **40 minutes** on Meat/Stew. When the timer ends, **wait 15 minutes**, then vent the rest by hand. Hearty enough to hit the same craving. Lazy enough that I still make it.

Still one rule: **noodles stay out of the pressure cooker.** Boil them in plain water on the side.

**Knife-cut**, **egg noodles**, or whatever wheat noodle you keep around. Same pot rule for all of them.

Noodles in the braise turn to paste. One extra pot. Worth it.

## Why this lazy batch

**Broth-first.** I cut beef back to **1 kg** and pushed water to **2.4 L** (about **10 cups**). That is the most I run in an **8-quart** with this much meat. I stay under the max-fill line. Less beef per bowl, more soup to ladle. That is the trade I want for noodle soup.

**Best flavor upgrade: blanch and brown.** The basic recipe is dump-and-go. If you have another **10-15 minutes**, blanch and rinse the beef, pat it very dry, then brown it in batches. Wet supermarket cubes steam more than they brown - dry cubes pick up real color. The browned bits add noticeable depth to the broth. Extra pot and skillet. Worth it when I am not rushing.

**Blanch is optional.** A short boil, then a rinse, knocks the gray foam off the cubes. Cleaner broth. Extra pot. I skip it when I want dump-and-go. The dump-and-go pot still tastes like soup.

**Rough chop, not fine dice.** Quarter the yellow onion, chunk the tomatoes, slice the ginger thick. Garlic cloves peeled - smash them if you feel like it. **No green onion in the pot** - yellow onion already carries the allium base. I save green onion for the bowl.

**What 40 minutes does to the veg.** Tomatoes and garlic mostly melt into the broth - that is where a lot of the body comes from. Onion turns very soft; you may still see pieces when you ladle. Ginger stays fibrous but quiet. This is a broth pot, not a vegetable soup. Strain or ladle carefully if you want a cleaner bowl.

**Cubed beef only.** Supermarket stew beef - already cubed, usually chuck. Buy a **1 kg** package, dump it in, ladle straight into the bowl after pressure. At **40 minutes** the cubes are fork-tender - they break apart when you bite, but most pieces still hold shape in the ladle. Not mush. Past **60 minutes** on the same cubes they start going stringy and dry. Want more gelatin and a richer broth? Swap part of the stew beef for beef shank. **1 kg chuck** from the package is still my default.

**One pressure time: 40 minutes.** **40 minutes** under high pressure is where cubed chuck turns tender and the broth gets body. Collagen work happens there, not on release.

**One wait before vent: 15 minutes (timed partial natural release).** I do not wait for the pin to drop. That is full natural release - extra minutes, little extra benefit for this soup.

I also do not vent the second the timer beeps. At **2.4 L** that loses broth.

**Wait 15 minutes** with the lid on, then turn the valve and release whatever pressure is left. Same wait every time.

**Cube broth, not cartons.** I dissolve **3 beef bouillon cubes** in the **2.4 L** water instead of buying beef stock. Same lazy pantry move as my [Instant Pot arroz caldo](/blog/instant-pot-arroz-caldo/). Cubes plus soy and Worcestershire carry the savory base - no homemade stock, no overnight simmer.

A **6-quart** will not take this much liquid with a full kilo of beef - cut beef, water, and seasoning in half.

**1 tsp five-spice.** Powder only - no spice bags. **1 tsp** in **2.4 L** keeps five-spice in the background. Use **1 1/2 tsp** only if your blend is mild.

**Worcestershire at the start.** **1 tbsp** goes in with the water and soy - same move as my adobo. Fills the savory gap cube broth leaves without wine.

**Doubanjiang - optional upgrade.** Skip it and you still get a solid bowl: soy, tomato, five-spice. Add **25 g (about 1-1 1/2 tbsp)** when I want fermented chili-bean depth and heat - the Taiwan-inspired bump without a shelf of specialty jars.

**Taste after pressure.** **90 ml light soy** at the start is enough for soy to read clearly without oversalting. After venting, add another **15 ml soy** if the broth still tastes flat.

### If you care (optional)

Default below is weeknight batch - same **40 minutes** under pressure. Three escalations if you want more depth without rewriting the recipe:

- **Blanch and brown:** Best flavor upgrade - see optional steps below. Cleaner broth, real Maillard on dry cubes. **10-15 minutes** extra.
- **Stock not cubes:** Use **2400 ml beef stock (about 10 cups)** instead of water; cut to **1-2 beef bouillon cubes** or skip them. Same load and pressure time.
- **Strain the bowl:** Ladle through a fine-mesh strainer, or strain the pot once after venting. Keeps ginger fibers and onion chunks out of the bowl.

## My weeknight 8-quart batch

**Feeds 6-8 bowls.** Broth keeps two days in the fridge; freeze the rest. Boil fresh noodles each time.

### Ingredients

#### Beef and aromatics (rough chop)

- 1000 g cubed beef stew meat, about 2.2 lbs (supermarket stew beef; chuck cubes)
- 400 g yellow onion, about 1 large, peeled, roughly chopped
- 300 g tomatoes, about 3 medium, roughly chopped
- 20 g garlic, about 6 cloves, peeled
- 50 g fresh ginger, about 5 cm (2 inches), roughly sliced

#### Broth and seasoning

- 2400 ml water, about 10 cups
- 3 beef bouillon cubes
- 90 ml light soy sauce, about 6 tbsp
- 15 ml Worcestershire sauce, about 1 tbsp
- 12 g brown sugar, about 1 tbsp
- 1 tsp Chinese five-spice powder

#### Optional: More depth

- 25 g doubanjiang (spicy bean paste), about 1-1 1/2 tbsp - fermented chili-bean heat
- 15 ml neutral oil, about 1 tbsp (only if you blanch and brown)

#### Noodles

- 800 g dried noodles, about 4 bundles (about 100-130 g dried per bowl)
- knife-cut, egg noodles, lo mein, or similar wheat noodles (fresh noodles work too; follow package time)

#### Toppings, Optional

- 450 g baby bok choy, about 4 heads
- 60 g green onion, about 4 stalks, thinly sliced

### Notes before you start

**Dissolve the cubes first.** Pour the **2400 ml water**, add **3 beef bouillon cubes**, and stir until dissolved before the beef goes in. If you use **doubanjiang**, stir it into that liquid too so nothing thick sits dry on the bottom.

**Stay below max-fill.** **2.4 L** water plus the beef and veg should sit well under the **8-quart** max-fill line. Check before you seal the lid.

**Start light on soy.** **90 ml light soy** plus **3 cubes** and **Worcestershire** already stack umami and salt. Taste after venting. Add another **15 ml soy** if the broth still tastes flat - easier than fixing oversalted soup.

**No wine.** Tomatoes, soy, Worcestershire, and the beef itself carry most of the flavor. Taste after venting - see Step 3 if something is off.

**Timed partial natural release.** Set a timer when HP ends. Do not touch the valve until **15 minutes** pass, then release remaining pressure manually. Not full natural release. Not immediate quick release.

**Blanch water is trash.** If you blanch, discard that water. Do not add it to the Instant Pot. The gray foam is what you were trying to leave behind.

## How I run it

### Optional: Blanch the beef (more depth)

Place **1000 g cubed beef stew meat (about 2.2 lbs)** in a saucepan. Cover with cold water.

Bring to a boil over high heat. Boil until gray foam rises, **2-4 minutes**.

Drain. Rinse the cubes under cold water until the water runs clear. Discard the blanch water.

Pat the cubes dry if you are going to brown them. Skip the next optional step if you are not.

### Optional: Brown after the blanch (best flavor upgrade)

Only after the blanch and rinse. Wet cubes will steam.

Heat a skillet over medium-high heat. Add **15 ml neutral oil (about 1 tbsp)**.

Brown the rinsed, patted-dry cubes in two batches, **2-3 minutes** per side, until they pick up color.

Transfer the browned cubes to the Instant Pot.

Add **60 ml water (about 1/4 cup)** to the skillet, scrape the browned bits, and pour that liquid into the Instant Pot.

### Step 1: Prep and load the pot

Roughly chop **400 g yellow onion, peeled (about 1 large)** and **300 g tomatoes (about 3 medium)**. Slice **50 g fresh ginger (about 5 cm / 2 inches)**. Peel **20 g garlic (about 6 cloves)**.

Pour **2400 ml water (about 10 cups)** into the Instant Pot.

Add **3 beef bouillon cubes**, **90 ml light soy sauce (about 6 tbsp)**, **15 ml Worcestershire sauce (about 1 tbsp)**, **12 g brown sugar (about 1 tbsp)**, **1 tsp Chinese five-spice powder**, and **25 g doubanjiang (spicy bean paste) (about 1-1 1/2 tbsp)**, if using. Stir until the cubes dissolve.

Add **1000 g cubed beef stew meat (about 2.2 lbs)** unless the cubes are already in the pot from browning. Add the chopped onion and tomatoes, **20 g garlic, peeled (about 6 cloves)**, and **50 g fresh ginger, roughly sliced (about 5 cm / 2 inches)**.

Stir once so everything is evenly wet. Do not stir again after the lid goes on.

### Step 2: Pressure cook

Seal the lid.

Cook on:

- High Pressure (Meat/Stew): 40 minutes
- Timed partial natural release: wait **15 minutes** with the lid on, then release remaining pressure manually

Do not vent immediately when the timer ends. Do not wait for the float valve to drop on its own.

### Step 3: Finish the broth

Taste the broth before you bowl it. Adjust in small steps - you cannot take soy or salt back out.

| If it tastes… | Try |
|---------------|-----|
| **Flat or thin** | **15 ml light soy sauce (about 1 tbsp)**; simmer on Sauté **5-8 minutes** uncovered to concentrate |
| **Not salty enough** | Pinch of kosher salt, then taste again |
| **Too salty** | Ladle into bowls as-is; dilute leftovers with hot water when you reheat |
| **Not beefy enough** | Dissolve **1 extra beef bouillon cube** in a ladle of hot broth, stir back into the pot |
| **Not sweet enough** | **5 g brown sugar (about 1 tsp)** |
| **Missing heat or depth** | **15 g doubanjiang (about 1 tbsp)** on Sauté, or **chili crisp** at the bowl |
| **Too perfumey (five-spice)** | Dilute with **120 ml hot water (about 1/2 cup)**; note for next time: use **1/2 tsp** five-spice |

Strain the broth or ladle carefully if you do not want big veg pieces in the bowl. Leave softened garlic in the pot.

Keep the broth warm on Low Sauté or Warm.

### Step 4: Cook the noodles

Bring a large pot of water to a boil.

Cook **800 g dried noodles (about 4 bundles, about 100-130 g per bowl)** until tender - knife-cut, egg noodles, or similar. Follow package time; egg noodles often finish faster than thick knife-cut.

Blanch **450 g baby bok choy (about 4 heads)** in the same water for the last 60-90 seconds, if using. Drain.

### Step 5: Assemble

Divide noodles among **6-8 bowls**.

Add beef and ladle broth over the top until the bowls are full.

Top with bok choy and **60 g green onion, thinly sliced (about 4 stalks)**, if using.

## Leftover broth is the point

**2.4 L** is more soup than one dinner. That is how a kilo of cubes beats a shop bowl.

**Next-day bowls.** Broth and beef keep **two days** in the fridge. Boil fresh noodles each time. I have left noodles sitting in leftover broth. Paste. Same rule as night one.

**Freeze the rest.** Portion into containers. Later: thaw, boil noodles, ladle. No second 40-minute cook.

**Rice.** Cook rice in leftover broth instead of water. Eat leftover beef over that rice, or put a smaller noodle bowl next to it.

**A mug.** Reheat a cup of broth when you do not want a full bowl. That is still the kilo stretching.

Do not pour it out.
