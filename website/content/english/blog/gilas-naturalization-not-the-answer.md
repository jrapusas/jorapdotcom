I'm glad we still care about **Gilas**. A full **Philippine Arena** on a World Cup night is not nothing. Thirty-eight thousand people showed up because the home team was on the floor. That noise still gets me.

Filipinos do not treat basketball like a side hobby. Barangay courts fill up on Sundays. **UAAP** and **PBA** games travel group chats. Kids grow up saying they want to play like **Robert Jaworski** or **June Mar Fajardo**. Plenty also want the **NBA** - **Jordan Clarkson** in the league, **Kai Sotto** still chasing it. That dream can pave the way for the next Pinoy. It is not the same ladder as **Gilas**.

For **Gilas** on the world stage, the target is **FIBA** - a **World Cup** berth and a return to the **Olympics**. We still rank ourselves against the rest of **Asia** even when the honest answer hurts. Best in the region is the dream. It has been for decades.

I'm also glad someone finally said what fans already know. **FIBA naturalization** has gone too far. This breakdown makes the case well:

Naturalization is not the **program** for **Gilas**. I am not calling for a ban on every import.

**Marcus Douthit** and **Andray Blatche** helped us reach two World Cups. **Justin Brownlee** nearly got us to the **2024 Olympics**. I am glad for those nights.

The problem is what we reach for next. **FIBA gives every team the same one naturalized slot** - **Japan**, **Korea**, **Lebanon**, and **Australia** included. It does not separate you in **FIBA Asia** qualifying. No Olympics since **1972**. **Australia** is in our qualifying group now.

## What FIBA actually allows

FIBA does not hand out passports. A country's government does. FIBA just leaves **one roster spot** for a player who got citizenship late and has no real ties before age 18.

That is the slot the **Samahang Basketbol ng Pilipinas (SBP)** keeps filling. One import big. One hope that height fixes what the pipeline did not.

Strip away the paperwork. Naturalization is simpler than that. It is a player's **commitment under a flag**. Not a skill sheet. Not a congressional bill. It is a promise to stand for this country when the FIBA window opens. That is what fans think they are buying. Too often the federation rents a player and hopes the commitment shows up too.

Filipino-heritage players who got a passport before 16 can count as locals. Miss that window and FIBA treats you like an import anyway. That is why **Jordan Clarkson** and **Christian Standhardinger** still use the naturalized slot even when fans treat them as "ours."

That is a different story from hired **PBA** imports. **Clarkson** and **Standhardinger** grew up with Filipino ties. I am not grouping them with **Blatche** or the passport-bill cycle for imports. This piece is about speeding up hired stars when the player path is thin. It is not about heritage players fans already claim.

Back in **1985**, the rule was looser. FIBA let **two** naturalized players. The Philippines used both and won **FIBA Asia** in Kuala Lumpur. After that era, the federation mostly stopped until **Smart Gilas** started hunting again in **2009**.

## How we reached the World Cup and the Olympics

We chase two prizes. A **FIBA World Cup** spot and a return to the **Olympics**. Those are the world stages FIBA actually gives a national team. The World Cup is the more reachable target - we qualified competitively in **2014** and **2019**, and co-hosted **2023**. The Olympics is the bigger dream and the harder ask. FIBA only sends a handful of teams from our part of the world.

Our last Olympic game was **1972** in Munich. That is more than fifty years of waiting. The country kept believing anyway.

Below is how we got in when we did. I skip **2023**. We co-hosted and qualified automatically. I also note which **naturalized** players were on those runs. I note who blocked us when we fell short.

### FIBA World Cup - how we qualified (competitive paths)

| World Cup | How we qualified | Naturalized player on the run? |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| **1954, 1959** | Early-era direct entries | None |
| **1974** | Won **1973 ABC** in Manila (**10-0**, beat **Korea** in the final) | None - all homegrown |
| **2014** | **2013 FIBA Asia** silver in Manila - beat **South Korea** **86-79** in the semis; top three went to Spain | **Marcus Douthit** - anchor all tournament |
| **2019** | **FIBA Asia-Pacific qualifiers** - **7-5**, **best fourth** in Asia (squeaked in on the last window) | **Andray Blatche** - naturalized slot through the quali cycle |

**Near-miss with naturalized help:** **Jeff Moore** and **Dennis Still** won **1985 FIBA Asia** in Kuala Lumpur. That earned a **1986 World Cup** berth. The federation gave up the slot for political reasons. Imports won Asia. We never played in Spain.

**Not a competitive quali:** **2023** co-host auto bid (Manila) - excluded here on purpose.

### Olympics - how we tried to get back

| Cycle | Path | Naturalized player | Result |
|-------|------|-------------------|--------|
| **1972 Munich** (last time we played) | **1971 ABC** silver in Japan - top two went to Olympics | None | **Qualified** - finished 13th in Munich |
| **2012 London** | **2011 FIBA Asia** - needed top three | **Marcus Douthit** | **Missed** - **Jordan** beat us in the semis; **South Korea** **70-68** in the third-place game |
| **2016 Rio** | **2016 FIBA OQT** in Manila - winner takes Olympic spot | **Andray Blatche** (**Jordan Clarkson** ruled out; SBP picked Blatche) | **Missed** - **0-2** vs **France** and **New Zealand** |
| **2021 Tokyo** | **2021 FIBA OQT** in Belgrade (invited after **New Zealand** withdrew) | **Angelo Kouame** | **Missed** - lost to **Serbia** and **Dominican Republic** |
| **2024 Paris** | **2024 FIBA OQT** in Latvia - winner takes Olympic spot | **Justin Brownlee** - All-Star Five, led run to semis | **Missed** - lost to **Brazil** in the semis |

**Douthit** and **Blatche** genuinely helped us reach the **World Cup**. **Douthit**, **Blatche**, **Kouame**, and **Brownlee** all carried Olympic bids. None got us back to the Games.

**Brownlee** came closest in **2024**. We were two wins shy after a fifty-two-year wait. We still did not qualify.

That is the gap between a shortcut that fixes one tournament and a program that ends the Olympic wait.

| **2010 World Cup** | **2009 FIBA Asia** QF | **Jordan** **81-70** (also beat us in **2007** QF) |

Naturalization did not remove any of them.

### The Oceania merge - same spots, harder neighbors

Before **2019**, **Australia** and **New Zealand** qualified through **Oceania**. It was mostly a two-team playoff between themselves.

**FIBA Asia** teams fought other Asians for World Cup spots. They did not meet the Boomers in a qualifying group.

FIBA merged the zones starting with the **2019** World Cup cycle.

**Seven** competitive spots still go to the combined **Asia-Pacific** region. A host takes one when named. **China** in **2019**. **Qatar** in **2027**.

The spot count did not grow. The schedule did.

We now play **Australia** and **New Zealand** home and away in the same standings as **Iran**, **Korea**, and **Jordan**.

That is a different problem than adding one naturalized big.

**Australia** went **10-2** in the **2019** second round and **11-1** in **2023**.

**New Zealand** took a **2023** spot too.

In the current **2027** cycle, **Australia** is already through. They are **7-0** in second-round **Group E**. **Gilas** sits **2-4** in the same group.

Chasing "best in Asia" on the court now means beating Olympic-medal talent with a steady export path - **Japan**, the **NBA**, somewhere that sharpens locals. Not one naturalized big and a thin roster behind him.

That is not the same as topping the old Asian-only table on paper.

**Who has qualified from this merged bracket since it started:**

| World Cup cycle | Teams that reached the tournament (competitive quali) | Notes |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------------------------|-------|
| **2019** | **Australia**, **Japan**, **Iran**, **New Zealand**, **South Korea**, **Jordan**, **Philippines** (best fourth, **7-5** with **Blatche**) | **China** hosted. We took the last open slot over **Lebanon**. |
| **2023** | **Australia**, **China**, **New Zealand**, **Iran**, **Jordan** | **Philippines** and **Japan** co-hosted. Only **six** competitive slots - **Lebanon** finished second in its group and still missed. |
| **2027** (ongoing, as of **August 2026**) | **Australia** and **Lebanon** clinched so far; **Qatar** hosts | **Gilas** trails **New Zealand**, **Iran**, and **Jordan** in **Group E**. Several spots still open. |

The pattern is clear. Oceania powers take spots every cycle.

**Philippines** only got in once in this format. That was **2019** as best fourth.

A naturalized center does not shrink **Australia**. It does not move **New Zealand** out of your group.

## What the shortcut actually bought us

I am not pretending the imports did nothing. The summary above already thanked **Douthit**, **Blatche**, and **Brownlee**. This section is the cost.

Since **2009**, the SBP has tried **more failed candidates than long-term picks**. Giles. Sampson. Vucicevic. McGee hype that never landed. Austin. Smith. Each cycle costs time and fan hope.

**Blatche** showed up out of shape in later windows. The **2019 World Cup** went **0-5**. The **2023** home World Cup did not change the story. One import slot does not fix youth height, school programs, or a pro league that caps foreign players at one per team.

The YouTube video's North Macedonia example is the extreme end. **Six Americans in twelve months**. Six different flags, if you can call that commitment. We are not that bad. We still play the same game. Find the hole. Shop for a passport. Call it progress.

## Why a passport is not a program

Copying other countries is not a plan. **Japan**, **Korea**, **Lebanon**, and **Australia** can naturalize and still win because their local player path works first. We reach for a passport when the path is thin. That is the mistake.

If naturalization were the fix, **Gilas** would be ahead by now. We have been hunting imports since **Smart Gilas** in **2009**. The qualifying tables above show the gap. One hired big does not fix weak youth height, a thin frontcourt, or a **PBA** that develops locals behind one import per team.

That is the sign.

**Naturalization is not a competitive edge in FIBA Asia.** **Japan**, **Korea**, **Lebanon**, **Jordan** - they all naturalize. FIBA gives every team one slot.

It fails because we treat it like the program instead of a patch.

### FIBA Asia qualifiers - one bracket, harder road

**Gilas** does not qualify alone. **Australia**, **New Zealand**, **Japan**, **Korea**, **Lebanon**, and **Jordan** share the same **FIBA Asia** road. World Cup windows. **Asia Cup**. Same naturalized-player rule. Oceania merged into Asia for World Cup qualifying starting **2019**. That whole bracket counts now. It includes two countries we never had to beat in qualifying before the merge.

FIBA allows **one** naturalized player per roster. Most teams in that bracket use the slot. Some pair it with homegrown stars. We pair ours with a thin local frontcourt and hope the math works.

| Country / team | Naturalized player (recent) | Notes |
|----------------|----------------------------|-------|
| **Australia** | **Bryce Cotton** (also **Kevin Lisch**, **Matisse Thybulle**) | Boomers pick **one** per tournament. **Cotton** debuted in **2025** qualifiers vs **Gilas** in Perth |
| **New Zealand** | **Casey Frank** ( **2006-2014** ); slot often empty lately | Tall Blacks leaned on Frank for years; recent cores use **Webster**, **Fotu**, **Shea Ili** |
| **Japan** | **Josh Hawkinson** (also **Nick Fazekas** before him) | Naturalized bigs beside **Yuki Kawamura**, **Rui Hachimura**, **Keisei Tominaga** |
| **South Korea** | **Ra Gun-ah** (Ricardo Ratliffe) | Naturalized since **2018** |
| **Jordan** | **Dar Tucker** (after **Rondae Hollis-Jefferson**) | Swaps the slot when the last import leaves |
| **Lebanon** | **Dedric Lawson**; **DJ Funderburk** incoming | New naturalized bigs on a deep local core |
| **Qatar** | **Brandon Goodwin**; **Kendrick Nunn** pursued | Shopping NBA-level guards for the **2027** cycle |
| **Chinese Taipei** | **Brandon Gilbeck** | **7-foot** naturalized anchor since **2021** |
| **Bahrain** | **CJ Giles** (among others) | Same **CJ Giles** who tried out for Smart Gilas later played for Bahrain |

That is not a Philippines-only trick. It is **FIBA Asia** standard - from **Manila** to **Perth** to **Beirut**. Everyone in the bracket gets the same option. A passport does not move you past **Australia**, **Iran**, or **Jordan** in the standings. Knowing that does not tell us what **Gilas** should do. It only shows the shortcut is not an edge.

**Japan** does not win because Hawkinson exists. Japan wins because **B.League** development stacks with Hawkinson and **NBA**-level local players.

**Korea** keeps **Ra Gun-ah** and still builds around **Lee Hyunjung**. **Lebanon** adds **Lawson** on top of Cedars depth.

**Australia** naturalizes too. They still do not need it to beat us. The Boomers roll out **Josh Giddey**, **Dyson Daniels**, **Dante Exum**, **Josh Green**, **Patty Mills**, **Jack McVeigh**. Naturalization is a bonus pick, not the program. Their **NBL** feeds the national team. Ours mostly feeds import debates. In **2018**, they sent **Thon Maker** and **Lisch** in the same qualifying window against **Gilas**. Maker counted as local. He got citizenship as a teen refugee. Same paperwork games. Different depth behind it.

**New Zealand** shows the other path in the same bracket. They naturalized **Casey Frank** for a decade. Then they built toward **2022 FIBA Asia Cup** bronze with local guards and forwards. Would that fix **Gilas**? No. It only stacks on a player path that already works. We keep trying to skip straight to the stack.

We add **Brownlee** and still ask why the program feels stuck.

### Europe and the rest of the world too

The video above is not only about Asia.

**North Macedonia** cycled **six** naturalized Americans in about a year.

**Slovenia** hunts a new big every summer for **Luka Dončić**.

**Spain** won **EuroBasket 2022** with **Lorenzo Brown**. He is American. He had never played in Spain before he got the passport.

**France** fast-tracked **Joel Embiid**, then watched him play for **Team USA** against France at home.

**Italy** courted **Paolo Banchero** for years. He picked **Team USA** anyway.

### The American player path - Team USA first, another flag second

The deepest talent pool on earth is American. **Team USA** only takes twelve. Any U.S. pro with a real shot will try for that roster first.

When the final cut says no, most do not walk away from big tournaments. They look for another door.

**FIBA** leaves one naturalized slot open on dozens of teams. **Bahrain**. **North Macedonia**. **Qatar**. **Philippines**. And the rest. A second passport is often a backup plan. World Cup windows. **Olympics** qualifying. Continental tournaments. Whatever stage still fits their career.

**Embiid** and **Banchero** chose **Team USA** when they could. Good for them. For every star who picks **Team USA**, there are players who take the first flag that gets them back on a FIBA floor. That is not love of country. That is a career move.

That is why **North Macedonia** can cycle six Americans in a year. That is why **CJ Giles** tried **Smart Gilas** and later played for **Bahrain**. We are not the only country shopping for a passport.

A passport stop is not a program. Countries that win long-term still grow local players. Their pro leagues export players, not just import them.

### What that means for Gilas

Naturalization was never going to carry us alone. It was sold that way anyway - one big, one bill, one window saved.

A Pinoy in the **NBA** would lift the whole country. **Clarkson** already does in stretches. That is not a **Gilas** program either. You still need twelve who can qualify through **FIBA Asia** - World Cup windows, Olympic quali, the merged bracket with **Australia** in it.

Lebanon's **Lawson** and Japan's **Hawkinson** walk into windows built on years of local investment. We walk in with **Brownlee** and hope the local players around him grew up fast enough.

That is why I do not want the Philippines to treat naturalization as the plan. A national team is supposed to show what **your** basketball produces. Not the best hired player you could naturalize before the next window.

FIBA already tightened switching national teams after senior tournaments. Good start. The video argues for something stricter, closer to **FIFA residency rules**. Live in the country. Do not just sign papers between qualifying windows. I would take that over another emergency import.

**Lithuania** reportedly shut down rumors about **D'Angelo Russell** because veterans pushed back. They would rather lose with their own guards than win with a rental. I respect that more than another congressional bill for a **PBA** import.

## The long fix is not a passport

Naturalization is the fast lane. You need a big for the next FIBA window. You find a **PBA** import or an overseas pro. Congress passes a bill. He plays. The crowd cheers. The youth system stays the same. You asked for commitment under the flag. You got a rental with a passport.

The long fix is **local talent**. Kids who grow through school leagues, **UAAP**, and a pro system that actually develops them. Not renting a finished product every season. They do not need a bill to prove they belong under the same flag the jersey wears.

That means fixing the **PBA**, not just the national team photo. One import per team is easy math for owners. It is bad math for growth. Local bigs fight for scraps behind a hired anchor. Guards learn to play around the import, not through a full five-man system built here. A league that treats its own roster like supporting cast will keep sending desperation to **Gilas**.

It also means loosening the clearance wall when a Pinoy wants an Asian league stint that builds **Gilas**.

Not only blocking it until he comes home on the league's terms.

Then it shops for that same import's passport when the FIBA window opens.

## Send Pinoys abroad as the import

Here is the other side I wish we talked about more.

Filipinos already leave for **Japan**, **Korea**, and other Asian leagues.

**Kiefer Ravena**, **Thirdy Ravena**, **Robert Bolick**, and **Kai Sotto** are names fans already track.

**Sotto** is the clearest recent bet on the **NBA** chase from here. In those leagues a Pinoy is often the **Asian import** or the reinforcement. They are not the local fighting for minutes behind someone else's hired star.

### The PBA is strict when Pinoys leave for Asia

The **PBA** does not make that path easy. A player on a live **Uniform Players Contract** is bound to his mother team and the league. He cannot join **Japan B.League** or **KBL** clubs without clearance.

The usual chain runs like this. The foreign league writes to **SBP**. **SBP** asks the **PBA** when the player has a live deal. The **PBA** checks with the mother team. The Board of Governors decides. **FIBA** licensing follows the same clearance line.

**You still need SBP even if you never signed with the PBA.**

Skip the draft. Leave **UAAP** or college. You are not free to sign in **Japan** on your own.

**FIBA** treats **SBP** as the home federation for every Filipino pro. A foreign club cannot register you without a **FIBA** letter of clearance. That request lands on **SBP**. The federation checks whether you owe a live deal in the Philippines. A **PBA** contract. Another pro league. Or nothing on paper at all.

Why does **SBP** still gate it?

**FIBA** rules first. The member federation for the Philippines must confirm you are free to transfer. No **SBP** reply, no legal stint abroad on **FIBA** books.

**Gilas** comes second. **SBP** runs the national program. It needs to know where its players are licensed. It needs them reachable for FIBA windows.

Domestic order comes third. The **PBA** is not the only watcher. **SBP** still speaks for Philippine basketball when a Pinoy signs abroad.

**Thirdy Ravena** took that path. He skipped the **PBA** draft and went to Japan from **UAAP**. He did not need a **PBA** team release. He still went through **SBP** and **FIBA**. **Al Panlilio** said he had no team or league hook. Different circumstances from **Kiefer**. Still federation business. Still a **Gilas** conversation before he left.

**Kiefer Ravena** learned the **PBA** layer the hard way in **2021**. He signed with Shiga before the **PBA** cleared it. Commissioner **Willie Marcial** said he had to honor his **NLEX** deal. The board rejected the move at first.

[Clearance came later](https://www.pba.ph/news/ravena-cleared-to-play-in-japan-b-league) only after he finished the Philippine Cup. He agreed to one year abroad and promised to return to **NLEX**. Marcial said the case would not set a precedent. **NLEX** was fined.

**Ray Parks** got a smoother yes when **TNT** and chairman **Ricky Vargas** signed off.

[ESPN reported](https://www.espn.com/basketball/pba/story/_/id/35813046/in-face-lopsided-losses-easl-pba-falling-behind) the **B.League** wrote to **SBP** first. Same rule. Different room.

Even when a contract ends, mother teams often keep signing rights. A Pinoy who grows in **Japan** does not automatically return as a free agent. The **PBA** can block or delay the stint that would sharpen him for **Gilas**.

So the league is strict about Pinoys in Asian leagues. It is fast about naturalizing **PBA** hired imports for the national team. That order feels upside down to me.

Is the competition actually tougher than the **PBA**? At the top, yes. We have the scores to show it.

In **2023 EASL Champions Week**, both **San Miguel** and **TNT** went winless against **Japan B.League** and **KBL** clubs. San Miguel lost **96-68** to Ryukyu and **142-87** to Anyang. That is a **55-point** gap against a Korean team. TNT dropped games to Utsunomiya and Seoul by **33** and **11**.

[ESPN](https://www.espn.com/basketball/pba/story/_/id/35813046/in-face-lopsided-losses-easl-pba-falling-behind) framed the mood plainly. If the best **PBA** teams get crushed like that, the gap is real. Japan runs faster systems and often fields **two** imports. Korea puts more talent in a smaller league. **China's CBA** sits above us in depth too.

I am not saying every minute abroad beats every **PBA** night. **CSB coach Charles Tiu** pushed back on the size of the gap. Local vs local, he thinks we are closer than the scores looked. Prep and travel were unfair. Fair. I still trust the pattern. Pinoys who stay in Japan for years say the pace and physical play sharpened them. The **B.League** trains more modern and faces you with better imports nightly than most **PBA** weeks do.

### Build around Pinoys abroad

Here is the part that stings for **Gilas**.

**Kiefer Ravena**, **Thirdy Ravena**, and **Kai Sotto** left to play as Asian imports in **Japan** and **Korea**. They got tougher minutes and a bigger stage. They could come back to **Gilas** as locals.

So what does the federation do when a FIBA window looms? It does not build around those Pinoys. It naturalizes someone else's **PBA** import and burns the one FIBA slot every rival already uses too.

How do you sell **Gilas** as the dream when the player path points the other way? You reach for another passport bill instead.

The better play is obvious and harder. Develop locals. Build a **PBA** that prepares players for **Japan** or **Korea**. Let **Gilas** draw from Pinoys who proved themselves there. They play as locals, not as our one naturalized slot.

Taiwan is a different case. Filipinos go there for roles too. I would not put every Asian league in one "tougher than home" bucket. **Japan** and **Korea** are the clearest step up. That is the path I mean.

I am not saying every local player must go abroad. I am saying the path exists. It beats another cycle of naturalizing someone else's finished big. Welcome them back with real minutes when they return.

---

## What I would rather see

I would rather lose an honest game with a home-grown core than win a shallow one and pretend the system works.

That does not mean ban every heritage player or every kid who grew up abroad with a Filipino parent. It means stop treating **naturalization** as the main tool whenever we fall short.

Fund height in schools. Expand the youth player path beyond **Metro Manila**. Reform the **PBA** so locals get real development minutes, not just import support duty. Treat Asian leagues abroad as part of the player path, not a shameful exit. If you must naturalize someone, pick one player for a full cycle. Do not swap names every time the last one flies home out of shape or injured.

I am still showing up when Gilas plays. I still want the arena loud. I just do not want the federation to keep calling a passport the program.

We have been naturalizing players since **Moore and Still** in **1985**. Imports helped two World Cup spots. None brought back the Olympics. Glad we have fans who still show up loud. Glad some imports gave us real nights. The long answer is still local talent and a harder domestic league. Pinoys can prove themselves abroad.

**Japan**, **Korea**, maybe the **NBA** - that is the path. **Gilas** can chase World Cup berths and an Olympic return there. Not another passport bill for a **PBA** hire. I am still grateful when the arena roars anyway.
