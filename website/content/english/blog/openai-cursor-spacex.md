I read [OpenAI's post about Cursor](https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex/) recently. Glad I already picked a default model and stopped treating the picker like a buffet.

**OpenAI says it will wind down the contract that puts its models inside Cursor.** Shutoff date: **November 12, 2026**. That's the longest notice their deal allows. The stated reason is not "Cursor got worse." It's **SpaceX acquired Cursor**, and OpenAI does not trust Elon Musk's companies to honor terms of service. They point at **Twitter/X** breaking contracts after the takeover and **xAI** violating OpenAI's ToS, which Musk admitted under oath earlier this year.

I use Cursor to code - Hugo layouts, templates, scripts on this site. I'm not cheering for anyone's lawyers. I'm also not panicking. OpenAI gave Cursor until **November 12**. That is enough runway for Cursor to line up replacements before the menu shrinks.

**Composer 2.5** is the model I reach for. The limits on Cursor's own bucket are generous enough that I rarely touch anything else. If OpenAI models vanish from the menu, my coding workflow probably does not change much.

---

## What OpenAI actually said

Short version, from their side:

- Cursor has had OpenAI models for **about four years**.
- The custom contract includes a **change-of-control exit**. SpaceX buying in triggered it.
- OpenAI is holding the cutoff to the **latest date the contract allows**.
- They are **not promising future models**. They name **Astra** as the upcoming one they want tighter control over.
- They say they still respect Cursor's team and product.

Fair. Also very corporate. The post spends more words on Musk and contract history than on what developers should do on **November 13**.

That gap is the story for me.

---

## A short history of Musk vs OpenAI

The Cursor notice did not come out of nowhere. **Musk and OpenAI have been fighting in public for years.**

**2015:** Musk helps launch **OpenAI** as a **nonprofit** research lab. The pitch: safe AI for everyone, not locked inside one company.

**2018:** Musk wants **more control** - a majority stake or folding OpenAI into **Tesla**. The other founders, including **Sam Altman**, say no. Musk **leaves the board** in February. OpenAI later adds a **capped-profit** arm so it can raise real money.

**2022-2023:** **ChatGPT** blows up. OpenAI becomes the name everyone knows. Musk starts **xAI** in **July 2023** - his own AI shop and a direct rival.

**2024:** Musk **sues OpenAI**, saying they broke the founding promise by chasing profit and tying up with **Microsoft**. He drops a state case, then **re-files in federal court**. The fight spreads: **injunctions**, **countersuits**, antitrust claims, nonprofit-to-for-profit drama.

**2025:** A judge calls Musk's "irreparable harm" claim a **stretch** and denies an injunction. **OpenAI countersues**, saying Musk is trying to slow a competitor. In **October 2025**, OpenAI closes a **for-profit recap** (**OpenAI Group PBC**) while the original nonprofit keeps a stake.

**May 2026:** A federal **jury in Oakland** rules Musk **waited too long to sue**. OpenAI wins. Musk says he will **appeal**.

Through all of it, Musk keeps **posting**, **suing**, and building **xAI**. OpenAI keeps shipping models and lawyering up. Neither side acts like the other is a neutral partner.

That is the backdrop when **SpaceX buys Cursor** and OpenAI emails developers about **terms of service**.

---

## This reads like a proxy war

OpenAI frames the move as **safety and terms of service at scale**. Maybe. After the timeline above, it still lands like **business conflict dressed as principle**.

SpaceX now owns **X**, **xAI**, and **Cursor**. The Cursor notice fits the same lane: **hurt a Musk-linked product without naming Cursor as the enemy**.

I'm a developer, not a party to that feud. When a vendor email opens with "we care deeply about developers," I read the rest carefully. When it closes by blaming another billionaire's portfolio, I notice who gets left holding the bag.

**The people who liked GPT inside Cursor** did not acquire SpaceX. They just picked a model in a dropdown.

If the real worry is misuse of **Astra**, say that plainly and offer a path for Cursor users who are not SpaceX. They did not. They picked the nuclear option the contract already allowed.

## Why I stay on Composer 2.5

I did not come to Cursor for **GPT-4** branding. I came for **an agent that reads the repo, proposes diffs, and lets me reject bad patches before they land**.

**Composer 2.5** is the first model that made that coding loop feel reliable. Not perfect. Sufficient. I stopped opening the model menu every session hunting for a smarter option that might hallucinate less on the next file.

Cursor's **usage limits** help too. On Pro I watch one main bucket (**Cursor Models**, which includes Composer) and keep **on-demand spend off**.

I am not starving for tokens on the model I already use.

What I want next is simple: **Composer 3** at the **same rates as Composer 2.5**. Not a new tier. Not a surprise quota squeeze. Just a better default in the bucket I already pay for.

So OpenAI's exit is less "my IDE is broken" and more "one tab in the picker goes gray." I might not notice for a week.

## I would not miss OpenAI models in Cursor

Honest take: **if GPT-family models disappear from Cursor tomorrow, I would shrug.**

It reminds me of **Disney and other studios pulling their shows off Netflix**. Big headline. Lots of angry threads. I did not care. I do not live on Netflix. **YouTube is enough** for what I actually watch.

Same shape here. OpenAI taking its models out of Cursor is a licensing fight, not a coding emergency.

**Composer is my YouTube** - the model I already use daily inside the IDE. I might still switch to **GPT in Cursor** for a second opinion on a tricky diff. I do not depend on it. I do not use AI for much outside coding anyway. This fight is about **which models sit in the editor**, not whether I use AI at all.

OpenAI still wins plenty of general chat apps. I am not writing this from one of them. Inside an IDE, the integration matters more than the logo on the weights. Cursor's post-training on Composer is the product. A raw GPT slot in the same sidebar was never the reason I paid.

## Why I will not use Claude for personal projects

**I will not use Claude for personal coding projects.** That is a hard line for me - not a ban on AI everywhere.

Personal means repos I own for my own work - **jorap.com**, my notes garden, Hugo layouts, scripts on this site, side projects I maintain myself. Not in Cursor for that code. Not in another coding assistant. Not for commits on personal repos.

At **work**, Claude is fine. The company pays for tokens there. AI is still helpful on client code. I just need to learn to spend **their** tokens wisely - not spray prompts on every small question.

**Digital watermarking** is why I draw the line at home. Anthropic has talked about marking AI-generated text so systems can detect it later. I do not want that fingerprint on source files I keep long-term. Maybe the risk is overstated. Maybe it only applies to certain outputs. For **my** personal code, I still walk away.

So when OpenAI models leave the Cursor picker, **Claude is not my personal backup**. One less third-party slot is not a loss for the repos I own myself.

I use AI heavily for coding on personal projects. I just keep it inside Cursor, mostly on **Composer**. SpaceX and OpenAI can fight over the rest.

---

## What happens after November 12

I am not planning a migration drama. Cursor has until **November 12** to land replacements - new partners, more **Composer** capacity, whatever fills the OpenAI gap. That is enough time. I am not worried the picker goes empty on day one.

1. **Keep Composer 2.5 as default** until **Composer 3** lands at the **same rates**.
2. **Ignore the model picker** unless a task fails twice. Same rule I use now.
3. **Watch whether limits change** if demand piles onto fewer providers. That is the real user-facing risk, not "I lost GPT-5."

If you live inside **OpenAI models in Cursor**, this hurts more. Fair. My stack is narrow on purpose.

---

## Still on Composer

Big AI vendors are **consolidating and feuding**. Cursor sits in the crossfire because **SpaceX bought it**. OpenAI's post is polite. The move still feels like **using developers as leverage** in a fight that is not ours.

I'm glad **Composer 2.5 and generous limits** were already enough before the headline dropped.

**Composer 3 at the same price** is the upgrade I am actually waiting for. If the picker shrinks, I will keep coding the same way. I'm glad the IDE won me on **workflow**, not on whose API key is plugged in behind the curtain.
