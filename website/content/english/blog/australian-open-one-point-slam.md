US Open Fan Week is on again: free grounds, qualifying, ticketed nights in Ashe before the main draw starts Sunday. I am glad the Slam calendar makes room for that week. The pre-tournament event that still lives in my head, though, ran in Melbourne in January: the **Australian Open One Point Slam**.

---

## What the One Point Slam actually is

Tennis Australia runs it during opening week, a few days before the Australian Open main draw. The rules exist only for this event:

- **One point** decides each match. Win it, advance. Lose it, go home.
- **Rock, paper, scissors** picks server and returner (a coin toss in 2025).
- **Pros get one serve.** Amateurs get two.
- **Mixed draw:** men and women, amateurs and pros, one knockout bracket.

No first set. No change of ends mid-game. No twenty minutes of trick shots. Every walk-on asks the same thing: *what happens on this one point?*

This is **sudden death in tennis**. Lose the point, lose the match. No second serve to bail you out if you are a pro. A tour player cannot unload a flat first serve the way they would on break point in a real match. One miss on the line, one net cord, and you are done. Amateurs still get two serves, but the crowd knows the pro standing on the baseline has no cushion.

That changes how the point plays. You hear **gasps** on balls that clip the line or look wide and land in. The kind of near-miss that would just mean "play on" in a normal game. Here it is the whole match. Returners cannot swing for a winner on every ball either. One wrong read and the bracket moves on without you.

If you love **board game mechanics**, this will click fast. It is a **single-elimination bracket** where one bad roll ends your run. Rock-paper-scissors is the setup phase. The serve rule is the handicap that keeps amateurs in the game. No best-of-three, just **one decisive moment** and the table flips. That is why it feels exciting in a way normal exhibition tennis does not. The format is the fun.

## A short history: pilot, then a leap

**2025 (inaugural):** Kia Arena, afternoon, about 74 minutes end to end. **32 players:** 16 amateurs (eight men, eight women, ages 15-72) and 16 pros. Five rounds, 31 one-point matches. Andrey Rublev was the only top-10 man in the field; he faulted on his only serve. Amateur **Paul Fitzgerald**, 56, reached the semifinals. The final was pro vs pro: **Omar Jasika** beat **Priscilla Hon**. Small crowd by Rod Laver standards, but Tennis Australia saw the format had legs.

**2026:** They brought it to another level.

- **Rod Laver Arena**, night session, **sold out**
- **Million-dollar prize** for the winner (up from a much smaller pot in 2025)
- **Famous headliners:** Carlos Alcaraz, Jannik Sinner, Iga Swiatek, Coco Gauff, Naomi Osaka, Alexander Zverev, and more. Plus a wider amateur path through club events and a December qualifying draw
- **48 players**, top pro seeds with byes, **eight celebrity wildcards** (pop star, streamer, TV hosts, AFL player, jockey). A club grant for the winner's home club

Same core rules: amateurs still get two serves, pros still get one. Bigger stage, bigger names, bigger stakes.

## What happened in 2026

Amateur **Jordan Smith**, a club coach and New South Wales state champion, won the million-dollar night. That outcome mattered. The night was built for stars. A local player walked off with the prize anyway. Perfect for a format that sells **anyone can win one point**.

Smith beat **Jannik Sinner** when the defending Australian Open champion put his only serve into the net. Smith had chosen to receive and never swung. He beat **Amanda Anisimova** in the quarter-finals on one point. In the final he faced **Joanna Garland**. She is a pro who had already beaten Zverev, Kyrgios, Sakkari, and Vekic on one point each. Smith won when her backhand sailed wide on the million-dollar serve.

The mixed draw is half the thrill. **Amateurs vs pros** on the same bracket is not a photo op. It is the whole event. A club coach can draw the world number two. A state champion can stand across the net from a Grand Slam finalist and need only **one point** to send them home.

So is **women vs men:** no separate bracket, same sudden-death rules.

**Iga Swiatek** beat **Flavio Cobolli** in a rally the crowd stood for.

**Maria Sakkari** beat **Carlos Alcaraz** when his drop shot died in the tape.

**Amanda Anisimova** knocked out **Daniil Medvedev**.

On this court, for this night, the draw does not care about tour gender splits. One point. Winner stays.

**Coco Gauff** faulted on serve against **Donna Vekic**. **Frances Tiafoe** did the same against **Iga Swiatek**. Pros who went out early stayed in the stadium and watched the rest on the TVs.

Tennis Australia filled **eight celebrity wildcards** with people who draw a crowd off-court. Taiwanese mandopop star **Jay Chou**. Twitch streamer **Loserfruit**. Australian TV presenters **Karl Stefanovic** and **Andy Lee**. AFL footballer **Bailey Smith**. Melbourne Cup-winning jockey **Damien Oliver**. Same bracket, same rules: rock-paper-scissors, one point, home if you lose. Chou became the night's best meme when Victorian state champ **Petar Jovic** aced him on serve. The pop star never touched a ball. Loserfruit lost to Queensland amateur Alec Reverente. Bailey Smith drew eventual winner Jordan Smith in an all-Smith first-round match. The celebrities were not there to win the million. They were there to sell the event as a **show**, and it worked.

Newspaper critics called it long and sometimes awkward. Fair. It still worked on TV because the gimmick is honest. Sinner did not lose a five-setter. He lost **one serve**. The arena gasped when his ball hit the net tape. Sudden death. No pretending you learned anything about a best-of-five Slam.

## Coming back for AO 2027

Tennis Australia is not treating 2026 as a one-off. The **AO 1 Point Slam returns for Australian Open 2027**. Same **million-dollar** winner-take-all prize on Rod Laver. A much wider grassroots path too.

What is planned:

- **Community events** at clubs, schools, and coaches across Australia (registration opened from **August 2026**)
- **State championships** on the weekend of **5-6 December 2026** (one male and one female champion per state and territory advance)
- **Grand final** during **Opening Week 2027** (state champs, qualifiers, pros, and celebrities in the same one-point knockout)
- **Club grants** (the winner's home club gets a **major tennis grant**; state champs earn a smaller grant for their club too)

Jordan Smith is already talking about hosting a community event at his own club to pull the next amateur in. The pipeline is the point: local court, state final, Rod Laver, one point for a life-changing night.

That is why 2027 feels exciting from the couch too. More amateurs will get a real shot at pros on the biggest stage. More mixed draws where a woman can end a man's night with one forehand. Same bonkers rules. Bigger funnel in.

Nothing is announced yet, but I would love to see **Alexandra Eala** in the 2027 draw. She was not in the 2026 One Point Slam. Melbourne already showed why Tennis Australia should care. Her 2026 main-draw debut packed **Court 6** past capacity. Pinoy fans queued across the grounds. Even Craig Tiley admitted crowd planning did not match the demand. Rod Laver was **sold out** for the One Point Slam.

Putting Eala in the 24 pro invites is the kind of move that fills seats and travels on social. Same reason Jay Chou got a wildcard. Except Alex actually plays tour tennis. She would need only **one point** to send a star home. She would draw a crowd from the Philippines and from Melbourne's huge Filipino community.

I am not holding my breath until Tennis Australia names the field. For a format built on amateurs, upsets, and diaspora noise, she is an obvious name.

## What I take as a TV fan (and a board game person)

I do not play tennis. Pickleball or badminton in a small room if I pick up a racket. I watch Slams from the Philippines on a screen. I play a lot of **modern board games** at home. The One Point Slam hits the same nerve: **tight rules, high stakes, one swing that ends everything**.

Pre-Slam programming earns my time when the format is the show. The One Point Slam in 2026 was amazing. Million-dollar prize. Alcaraz and Sinner on the poster. An amateur walked off with the check anyway, which is exactly the story you want from a mechanic like this.

I keep the clips above bookmarked for that reason: best-of first, then the upsets, then Smith's run. Exciting, honest, over before a normal set warms up. And with **AO 2027** on the calendar and more club paths opening up, I am glad this is coming back bigger. Not as a nostalgia night. As a format worth repeating.

I will watch Fan Week while the US Open warms up. I am still glad the best format-first pre-Slam event I know ran in January. Melbourne already has a date for the next one.
