I'm glad I started keeping notes on Alex Eala when the **2025 Miami Open** semifinal broke on my phone. For years her matches meant late-night streams and headline refreshes. You could follow the climb, but you were always on a screen. You were far from how fast she moves, how heavy the ball comes off her racket, how she fights when a point turns ugly. The excellence was real. It just happened in other countries.

That is the Filipino sports habit I keep noticing.
**Hidilyn Diaz** lifts in Tokyo, we cheer from Manila, then the motorcade.
**Carlos Yulo** sticks the vault in Paris, we get **Pagbibigay Dangal** on the street.
**Pacquiao** fights in Vegas.
GenSan throws a party when he lands.

We celebrate after our heroes already showed us what the best in the world looks like somewhere else.
We are very good at that part.

**December 8** at **Philippine Arena** in Bulacan tries a different order: an **exhibition match**. Sporting excellence in a huge arena, at home, from the hero while she is still playing at that level. Not a WTA ranking event. Not a thank-you parade after the medal.

I will most likely not be in the building. This is not a ticket pitch. It is a fan trying to name what that shift would mean.

In tennis, love means zero on the scoreboard. This December is about the other kind - and about whether the Philippines finally gets the live part, not only the celebration after.

## What got announced

Tatler is running **Alex Eala Live: Game. Set. Celebrate** on **December 8**, an **exhibition match** against **Janice Tjen**, not a tour stop or medal round. No ranking points on the line. Headlines will chase whether the building breaks the Cape Town attendance record. Michel Lamunière, chairman and CEO of Tatler Asia, said at yesterday's press conference: **"The record is not the reason for this event. Alex is."**

I am not in the promo business. I still think that line matters. The useful way to see it is not crowd count. It is whether Filipino fans get to **experience** a top-20 WTA player at the top of her run. Washington title. Wimbledon second week. The fight most of us have only watched from abroad on a screen. **Inside a huge home arena**. Against someone who can push back. Before anyone builds another float.

For a lot of fans it was the first time Alex was not a stream from Wimbledon or Miami. It was the first time **sporting excellence happened on home soil** instead of only being celebrated after. ## Celebrate after abroad. Rarely experience it live at home.

Filipino sports heroes usually come home to **thank-you events**.
Motorcade. Malacañang. Medals on stage.
Floats with a barbell or boxing gloves painted on the side.
**Hidilyn Diaz** got that after Rio silver and Tokyo gold.
**Carlos Yulo** got **Pagbibigay Dangal** in 2024.
Kilometers of flags from Aliw to **Rizal Memorial**.
Crowd copying his vault poses.
Caloy waving from a float.
Moving. Deserved. You felt pride.
The excellence itself already happened in another country.
You did not **experience** the double backflip in a huge arena here, in the same afternoon.
Not the speed, the land, the risk.

That is the gap I keep coming back to. Our default is **celebrate after**. Motorcade after Tokyo. Float after Paris. Watch party after Vegas. We rarely build a night where a home crowd in a **massive building** can **feel** how good a hero is while it is still happening. Footwork. Pace. Nerves under pressure. An opponent who can actually push back. Highlights kill that. A parade celebrates the medal. It does not put you by the court for ninety minutes.

**Pacquiao** did fight in the Philippines. Last one **July 2, 2006**, **Óscar Larios** at **Araneta** (*Mano-A-Mano*), before the **Cotto / Mosley** years most people mean when they say prime. Real home crowd (~**15,000**). Real fight, just not the Vegas-era big show. Nowhere near **Philippine Arena** size. His **Vegas prime** was abroad. GenSan threw watch parties when he landed. **Gilas** has played in Philippine Arena. July 2, 2018 was a qualifying game that ended in a brawl. Not a full night to study one Filipino athlete at their peak. **UAAP** and **PVL** give you live sport. College or club world. December 8 is a different kind of night: an **exhibition**. Alex, at her current level, against a **WTA-level opponent**, in a building sized for the whole country.

Tatler can break attendance records or not. What I am tracking is live **exhibition** tennis in a building this size, not another motorcade clip.

Rizal in January proved home-soil excellence can happen in a small room. December asks whether the same idea works when the building is sized for the whole country. The dome is not the story. **A hero playing top-level tennis inside it** is.

The difference from a **Gilas** qualifying game or a **Pacquiao** main event on TV: no single scoreline rewrites what she already did abroad. The love is not tied to one December night. The nerves still are. Even in a loss, anyone in the building would still have seen **that** level of tennis live, in the Philippines. I can hold both. I will not pretend only the win matters.

## The packaged night around the match

Tatler is not inventing the format from zero. The Grand Slams have run this pattern for years: **fan week before the main draw**. Youth tennis. Clinics. Celebrity practice hits. A main exhibition match. Fan zones and side events around the stadium. Dec 8 is Tatler trying the same idea in Bulacan around Alex.

**US Open - Arthur Ashe Kids' Day / Fan Week:** Decades of junior clinics, music, and practice hits in **Arthur Ashe** before the Open. The adult version now includes **Stars of the Open** - an **evening** session under the lights. Alex was literally in one **August 27** (see clips above): rising stars, exhibition sets, dads on court, packed stadium before round one.

**Australian Open - Opening Night:** Before the main draw, **Rod Laver** runs ceremony nights. Legends on court. Crowd in the seats. The building lit up like a concert dome.

**Wimbledon - Centre Court centenary (2022):** Legends back on grass for a **night** celebration - not a bracket match, still a full stadium under the lights.

That is the template Tatler is copying: **opening acts → fun match → main event**. Young Filipino talents first, then a **celebrity mixed-doubles exhibition** (lineup still **TBA**), then **Alex vs Tjen**. Lamunière talked about students, young players, and first-time spectators.

Who might show up for the **celebrity mixed-doubles** part? Nobody has been announced. I like that format anyway. Four names on court, exhibition rules, room for bad shots at the net without pretending anyone is a pro player.

Fame is not the same as **willing to play tennis on a hard court in front of fifty thousand people**. Neither is cheering Alex online. I have no idea who would say yes. If Tatler lines up media partners the way Philippine sports usually does, the celebrity list might need faces from **ABS, GMA, and One Sports**. Not one network's promo night.
